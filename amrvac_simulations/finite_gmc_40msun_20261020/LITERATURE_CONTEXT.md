# Cloud-scale GRB evidence versus local WR bubbles

These three 2001 Reichart papers form a linked argument, not three
independent measurements of the local progenitor-wind structure:

1. Reichart & Yost, [astro-ph/0107545](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0107545),
   treat optical nondetections as upper limits and compare optical, X-ray,
   and radio afterglows. They infer that many optically dark bursts are
   extinguished by dust in their surrounding cloud. Their environmental
   density interpretation depends on the adopted afterglow model; the
   paper notes a competing low-density fit for GRB 990123.
2. Reichart, [astro-ph/0107546](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0107546),
   models optical-flash dust sublimation and X-ray fragmentation with a
   canonical graphite/silicate grain distribution and constant-density
   cloud. Its approximate optical-depth clearing distance is
   `10 sqrt(L49) pc`, where `L49` is the isotropic-equivalent *optical
   flash* luminosity in units of `1e49 erg/s`, not gamma-ray luminosity.
   High extinction requires remaining path length and column. Turning
   the line-of-sight constraint into a cloud radius and mass additionally
   assumes a roughly spherical cloud with the burst near its center.
3. Reichart & Price, [astro-ph/0107547](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0107547),
   argue that a cloud meeting those size/mass conditions is more plausibly
   self-gravitating molecular gas than a pressure-confined diffuse cloud,
   and compare a Galactic-like molecular-cloud column distribution with
   the available X-ray absorption measurements.

These papers motivate a molecular-cloud *scale* and line-of-sight context
for some long GRBs. They do not show that a cold dense medium survives at
sub-parsec radii through a progenitor's main-sequence, RSG, and WR winds;
nor do they measure a WR termination shock or a thin GRB density wall.
That is the distinct problem investigated by the finite-GMC AMRVAC cases
in this bundle. At lower metallicity, the canonical dust prescription and
its fixed optical-depth-to-hydrogen-column thresholds need re-evaluation;
the numerical thresholds should not be transplanted unchanged.
